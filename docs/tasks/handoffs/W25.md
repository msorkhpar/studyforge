## Decisions

### 1. ⛔ The binding is a declaration, and the domain limit is why

`module-structure.md` says *enumerate the legal, never the illegal*, and bounds
itself: **enumerability is a property of the domain**. Two domains were
available and only one is writable.

| domain | enumerable? | what it would have produced |
|---|---|---|
| "filenames in this directory that are not handoffs" | ⛔ free text | an exclusion list, known-incomplete, one entry short next wave |
| **"kinds of document that may live in this directory"** | ⭐ **writable — there are five** | `DOCUMENT_KINDS`, and an undeclared document is *refused* |

⚠️ **And the naive binding was not merely fragile, it was already false.**
Measured 2026-09-10 on `e5bcc85`: the directory held **53** documents, **10** of
which owe none of the six sections — and ⛔ **8 of those 10 already carried
`— handoff` in their own titles**, because their authors started from the
template. So neither the filename nor the title could be read as a declaration;
both are free text and both had already drifted.

⭐ **The direction is declaration → filename, never back.** The stem must begin
with the first declared ID, so a handoff stays findable, and a handoff for two
tasks — four of them exist — declares both and is representable rather than
exempted.

### 2. ⭐ *none* is a third marker, because `0` had to force a sentence

Rubric §8a's own answer to zero — *"say in the review which this is"* — is
addressed to the reviewer, and ⛔ **Ruling 49 exists because that reviewer did
not run the grep.** Ruling 29's shape is the one that works: a finding **is** a
marked item, one spelling, an unmarked finding unrepresentable. So "nothing
outside my scope" gets the same treatment instead of a second number: a marker,
carrying at least 20 characters of sentence, which may not stand beside a real
finding. **Zero markers is now a build failure.**

### 3. ⭐ Where the marker *sits*, not how many there are

§8a compares `MARKED` to `LINES`, which catches two markers on one line and
nothing else. ⚠️ **Measured: `FND-04` line 290 carried one marker alone in a
prose sentence, and one equals one, so the rubric's own instrument passed it.**
The check asks whether anything but bullet, heading, numbering or emphasis
markup precedes the marker. ⭐ The permitted lead was **derived by reading all
199 marker lines on the tip**, not guessed — six shapes, all six asserted, so
the migration was two lines rather than a rewrite of every handoff.

### 4. ⭐ A fenced block is quoted material and is not read

⚠️ **Found by this rule going red on this very handoff.** A handoff that
documents the vocabulary — a transcript of the check firing, the migration table
below — necessarily contains the literal markers, and ⛔ **every one of them is
evidence rather than a claim.** Prose outside the fence is still read, and both
halves are asserted: a skip that never turned back on would make every marker
after the first fence invisible, which is the silent direction.

### 5. ⭐ `<TASK-ID>/<n>` is checked as a shape, and the legacy range is a pinned ceiling

The PO's ruling (`agent-protocol.md`, *A finding is numbered inside its own
document*) landed mid-task and rides in this commit. ⭐ **The half a shape check
can assert is the scope: the `<TASK-ID>` must be one the document itself
declared**, which needs no registry and no allocator — the same reason the
ruling refuses one.

⛔ **The legacy numbers are not migrated**, because a handoff is a record and a
check that demands a record be rewritten is a check this project forbids. So
`LEGACY_GLOBAL_MAX` is a **ceiling**: a bare number at or below it is
grandfathered, one above it must carry its document's ID.

⚠️ **And the ceiling is pinned from a measurement, not from the ruling** — see
`W25/6`. A test asserts the tree still holds nothing above it, so the constant
cannot quietly stop being true.

### 6. ⛔ Split into a package at 422 lines, at the seam this handoff had already named

⭐ **`W25/5` in the first draft of this document predicted the seam — *the
declaration and the contract are two subjects sharing one file* — and the
finding-ID rule hit the ceiling four hours later.** The split is exactly where
the prediction said, `__init__.py` at 259 and `contract.py` at 229, and the
dependency runs one way. ⚠️ **The prediction and the split landing in the same
wave is the only reason it cost nothing**; at the ceiling it would have been
discovered by somebody who only wanted to add a rule.

### 7. ⭐ Documents are exempted, modules are not — and the exemption is theirs to make

W20's rule generalises. This check reads **only** `docs/tasks/handoffs/*.md`, so
no module can be excused. Inside that directory, the exemption is not a list a
maintainer holds: **a document makes its own**, out loud, and one that makes
none is refused by name.

- ⭐ **`tools/tests/quality/test_init.py` caught the registration**, unprompted:

## Findings

### W25/1 `[structural]` The migration put 9 untriaged structural findings into the wave-open list

⛔ **Annotating the four unmarked handoffs made real work visible, and visible
work is owed to somebody.** `FND-03` ×3, `FND-06` ×2, `SK-01` ×3, `W14-W18` ×1
now carry the structural marker and appear in `grep -rn '\[structural\]'
docs/tasks/handoffs/`, which `agent-protocol.md` calls *the triage list, before
a wave*.

**Measured 2026-09-10:** `docker/dev/check python3 -m tools.quality` → 4
`handoff-findings` before the annotation; the nine markers after it.

⭐ **Cost named:** these are not new defects — they were always there, and
`SK-01`'s six were APPROVEd twice in that state. The rule *"a wave that begins
with an untriaged structural finding has decided to pay for it twice"* now has
nine entries it did not have yesterday, and they are the PO's to route. ⚠️ **The
triage may well be *accepted, historical* for most of them**; what is not
available any more is not deciding.

### W25/2 `[structural]` A rubric grep quoted as a quantity under-counts, and §8a is the third instance

⚠️ W20's finding 1 said this would happen again to anybody automating a rubric
clause, and Ruling 55 carried it. **This is the instance that proves it from the
other side:** §8a's `MARKED = LINES` is not merely a smaller number, it is
**structurally blind to a whole shape** — one marker alone in a prose sentence
satisfies `1 = 1`.

**Measured 2026-09-10:** `FND-04.md:290` — `every \[structural\] one is ruled,
scheduled, or explicitly open` — passes §8a's snippet and fails
`tools/quality/handoffs.py`.

⭐ **Cost named:** §7b's import grep and §1a's personal-data sweep are the two
rubric clauses still unautomated, and each will find more than its snippet does.
The generalisation worth ruling is one line: **a rubric snippet is a *lower
bound*, and a clause that states a count states the instrument that produced it.**

### W25/3 `[structural]` A declaration closes "undeclared" and not "misdeclared"

⛔ `**Kind:** survey` on a document that is a task handoff escapes the six
sections, and nothing mechanical can tell the difference — the closed set makes
*silence* unrepresentable, not *lying*.

**Measured:** `test_a_filename_that_looks_like_a_handoff_is_not_read_as_one`
asserts exactly this behaviour, deliberately.

⭐ **Cost named, and it is small on purpose:** the reviewer reads one line —
rubric §8's first question becomes *"does this document's `**Kind:**` say what
it is?"* — where before they had to remember to run a grep at all. ⚠️ Filing it
because `module-structure.md`'s own *"a guarantee does not extend to what sits
beside it"* applies to this check: the **undeclared** case is demonstrated, and
the **misdeclared** one is asserted nowhere.

### W25/4 `[local]` Rubric §8's snippet still binds `$TASK` to the filename

The hand-runnable form keeps `TASK=<TASK-ID>` and `head -1 | grep "# $TASK"`,
which is the binding this task replaced. ⭐ **Cost named:** it is annotated with
what it under-counts and a reviewer with a shell should still have one, so the
snippet is kept rather than deleted — but a reviewer who runs it on
`SF-10-survey.md` will get six false failures and should read the paragraph
under it first.

### W25/6 `[structural]` The legacy finding range is 20–62 on the tree and 20–58 in the ruling

⛔ **`BOARD.md` and `agent-protocol.md` both state the closed legacy range as
20–58. The merged tree runs to 62.** `SF-10` minted findings 59, 60, 61 and 62
on `feat/SF-10-unit-builder` and they merged in `40731e4`, after the PO measured
`e5bcc85` and before the ruling landed.

**Measured 2026-09-10, merged tip:**

```text
grep -rhoE '^(#{2,4} |\*\*)[0-9]+\.? `\[(local|structural)\]`' docs/tasks/handoffs/
  → highest bare number: 62   (SF-10.md, lines 102, 119, 128, 138)
```

⭐ **Pinning 58 would have red-lined a merged record**, which the ruling itself
forbids in the same paragraph — so `LEGACY_GLOBAL_MAX = 62`, measured, with a
test that fails if the tree ever exceeds it. ⚠️ **This is Ruling 55 landing on
the ruling that cited it:** the range was an instrument reading taken on a tip
that moved, and it is the *third* consecutive round in which a number written
into a ruling was a fact about a measurement rather than about the tree.

⭐ **Cost named:** one sentence in `BOARD.md`'s disambiguation block — *the
closed range is 20–62; `SF-10/59–62` were minted before the ruling* — and the
citations stay resolvable. ⛔ **Do not renumber `SF-10`.**

### W25/7 `[structural]` A bare number below the ceiling stays legal forever

⛔ The grandfather is by **value**, so a new handoff numbering its findings `1.`,
`2.`, `3.` passes — the `<TASK-ID>/<n>` form is enforced only *above* the
ceiling. ⚠️ **That is a deliberate under-enforcement and it is the price of not
rewriting records:** telling new from old needs either a snapshot list of the 54
existing documents (an open set, and D2's exact failure) or a grandfather
declaration on each one.

**Measured:** `test_and_the_closed_legacy_range_is_never_red` asserts the
permissive half on purpose, at 1, 20, 47 and 62.

⭐ **Cost named, and it is small and shrinking:** the collision the ruling exists
to prevent is two documents claiming the same *global* name, and that is closed
— a bare number above 62 is refused, so the sequence cannot be continued. What
remains legal is per-document numbering in the older spelling, which collides
with nothing. ⛔ **The closure, if it is ever wanted, is one line in
`DOCUMENT_KINDS`' neighbour: a `**Kind:**` declaration could carry `legacy
numbering` and the check could then demand the scoped form of everything else.**
Filing it so that is a decision rather than a gap.

### W25/8 `[structural]` The first author to meet the contract reached for it and spelled it differently

⭐ **`SF-12-survey.md` declared itself before this check ever ran on it** — its
author knew `W25` was coming and wrote, on line 3:

```text
**Document kind: `survey`.** ⛔ **Not a task handoff, and it does not owe the
six-section handoff contract** … ⚠️ Named here because `W25` is writing a check
on this directory and an undeclared document is what will trip it.
```

⛔ **The design worked and the spelling missed.** `KIND_MARKER` is `**Kind:**`;
this is `**Document kind: …**` — three words apart, and the check reported it as
undeclared, which is *correct* and also the least helpful true thing it could
have said to somebody who had already done the work.

**Measured 2026-09-10 on `1b2d993`:** 1 of the 2 documents the merge added had
attempted a declaration; the other had none.

⚠️ **This is `config.SIZE_EXCEPTION_MARKER`'s comment coming true on a second
marker** — *"a marker that differs by a hyphen or a capital passes the checker
and then fails review, which is the worst of the available outcomes"* — except
here it fails the checker, which is the better half of that trade and still
costs the author a round trip.

⭐ **Cost named, and it is a message rather than a rule:** the finding already
names the closed set of kinds; it should also say **the literal spelling** and
**where it goes**, because the reader who sees it has usually written *something*
already. ⛔ Not fixed here — `W25` is approved and this is a message change on an
approved surface, so it is filed for whoever next touches
`tools/quality/handoffs/__init__.py`. ⚠️ A near-miss spelling is evidence the
convention is being read, and the second author will make the same one.

### W25/5 `[local]` `tools/quality/handoffs/` — the seam this predicted, resolved

⚠️ **27 lines of headroom, and the fence rule took 13 of them after the module
was written.** ⭐ **Cost named:** the next rule added to it will not fit, and the seam is
already visible — the *declaration* (kinds, IDs, filenames) and the *contract*
(sections, markers) are two subjects sharing one file. Filing it so that split
is a decision rather than something discovered at the ceiling.

## For dependents

- ⛔ **Your handoff declares itself.** Second line: `**Kind:** task handoff —
  <TASK-ID>`. The floor fails you in the same run, not at review.
- ⛔ **Zero findings is written, never left blank.** The *none* marker plus the
  sentence saying what you looked at. A handoff that marks nothing fails.
- ⚠️ **Do not write the markers in backticks when you are talking *about*
  them** — that is a finding that does not exist. Use the words *local* and
  *structural*.
- ⭐ **A survey, a ruling record or a session log is legal here** and owes only
  its declaration. ⛔ **A sixth kind of document is one entry in
  `DOCUMENT_KINDS`** with the sentence saying what it owes — never an exclusion
  list, and never a filename pattern.
- ⛔ **Number your findings `<TASK-ID>/<n>`, starting at 1**, with the ID your
  own document declared. Bare numbers above **62** are refused — the global
  sequence is closed and there is no allocator, because a branch cannot hold one.
- ⚠️ **A handoff covering two wave items declares both** (`**Kind:** task
  handoff — W17, W19`), its title names both, and its filename begins with the
  first.
