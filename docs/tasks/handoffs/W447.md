## The cause

⛔ **`details.disclosure` carried `max-width: var(--measure)`, the prose measure**, so everything
inside an open disclosure was held to it. A worked solution is `details.disclosure > figure.code`
(`render/page/practice.py` emits it as *"Show a worked solution"*). ⭐ A code figure in the flow
has no cap and takes the full reading column, as `reading.css`'s own comment on
`main p, main li` says it should. ⚠️ **So the two differ only where the column is wider than the
measure**, and at 1280 or 720 they agree. That is why the harness's default viewport never saw it.

⭐ **The fix:** the disclosure is a container and keeps no measure of its own. Its paragraphs and
list items are still capped by `main p, main li`, the same as outside a disclosure. Only its two
feint rules and its code figures now span the column.

## Findings

### W447/1 `[structural]` — the reading page's seven vendored faces are blocked by its own CSP when served

⛔ **Every served reading page loses all its vendored type. The reader sees the fallback faces.**

- **What the page loads:** `render/pageassets/faces.py` writes seven `@font-face` rules into
  `page.css`, each with `src: url("data:font/woff2;base64,…")`. They are Charis 400, 400 italic
  and 700, Andika 400 and 700, and JetBrains Mono 400 and 700. ⭐ The fonts are inlined as `data:`
  URIs and are never fetched from any host.
- **What blocks them:** `serve/security.py`'s `POLICY_HEAD` sets `font-src 'self'`, and `'self'`
  does not match a `data:` URI. (`img-src` already lists `data:`, and `font-src` does not.)
- **Evidence** (served scratch clone, headless Chrome): seven `Network.loadingFailed` events, each
  `type: Font` and `blockedReason: csp`. Seven console `security` errors read *"Loading the font
  'data:font/woff2;base64,…' violates the following Content Security Policy directive…"*. That
  matches the seven the user's console showed. The seven blocked URIs are distinct, one for each
  face `faces.py` declares. When `document.fonts` was read after `fonts.ready`, it reported Charis
  400/700, Andika 400/700 and JetBrains Mono 400/700 as `error`, and Charis italic as `unloaded`.
  By count, its request is the seventh one blocked.
- **What the reader sees:** the declared fallbacks. Georgia (or the platform serif) for prose,
  Verdana for headings and the interface, and DejaVu Sans Mono or `monospace` for code. ⚠️ So the
  `W362` type is **not** what the served site shows. A wider fallback mono may also be why the
  user saw the solution scroll.
- ⭐ **Why no harness saw it:** the visual harness opens pages over `file://`, which carries no
  CSP, so the faces load there. Only a served page applies the policy.
- ⛔ **Not changed here, as instructed.** The candidate remedies are for the register to choose:
  `font-src 'self' data:`, or `page.css` referencing the faces as files under
  `.studyforge/assets/`. The second keeps the policy as it is.
- ⭐ **`[structural]` because it recurs for every corpus served, and because the harness's
  `file://` floor will hide the next served-only defect the same way.** Routed to the register
  for a ruling or a row.
