# W181, W192 — handoff

**Kind:** task handoff — W181, W192

**Status:** done

**What landed:**

⭐ **`tools/quality/ids.py` — Ruling 218's multi-id cell grammar, as ONE module both
packages import.** Its surface is `SEPARATORS` (`+` and `,`), `ROW_ID` (`W\d+`),
`parts(cell)`, `row_ids(cell)`, `is_row_id(text)` and `order(identifier) -> int | None`.
⛔ **It imports `re` and nothing else**, because it is read by
`tools/quality/board/register.py` AND by `tools/quality/handoffs/__init__.py`, and a
grammar that imported either would have to be imported by the other.

⭐ **`W192` — the three readers now read one grammar.** `register.identifiers` delegates
to `row_ids`, so every board reader of a multi-id cell inherits it — the register parser,
the In-flight parser, `dispatch.py`'s carrier declaration and `corroborate.py`'s unit
count. `declared_kind` splits with `parts` instead of on `,` alone. ⛔ **The grammar is
DECLARED in [`board.md`](../../conventions/board.md), beside what a register row may
carry**, and [`agent-protocol.md`](../../conventions/agent-protocol.md) points at it from
the `**Kind:**` bullet rather than carrying a second copy.

⭐ **`W181` — the ordering sites return where they raised.** `existence.py` gained
`unorderable(rows)`, `findings_for(rows, declared)`, `lines_for(rows, declared)` and the
rule code `handoff-row-id`; `existence_findings` and `existence_lines` are now the board
read in front of those two. ⛔ **No `int()` on a sliced id survives in that module.**

**Decisions:**

⛔ **`W181` clause 3's design decision, taken and recorded: BOTH clauses, because they
close different halves.** ⭐ **The shape is now a STATED PROPERTY where it is parsed** —
`row_ids` can only return `ROW_ID`, asserted over planted garbage — ⛔ **and the ordering
sites REPORT rather than raise anyway**, because `owing`, `findings_for` and `lines_for`
are public and a caller's rows are not the parser's.

⚠️ **The consequence is stated rather than discovered: the reporting arm is UNREACHABLE
from `check_handoff_existence`**, because the parser in front of it cannot construct a
malformed id. ⭐ **So the judging half was split into PURE FUNCTIONS OF THE ROWS
(`findings_for`, `lines_for`) and asserted through those** — ⛔ **an arm asserted only
through a walk that cannot produce its input is a check that could not fire, which is the
exact defect `W167` exists to close.** ⚠️ **`existence_lines`'s live sentence therefore
reads `none.` forever unless somebody hands it rows directly, and it says so out loud
rather than omitting the population** (Ruling 191).

⛔ **`W192`'s grammar reads MORE than it declares, and the direction was chosen.** ⭐ A
part is still read across its own whitespace, so `` `W20` and `W21` `` names BOTH ids to
every board reader and never the LAST — ⚠️ **which is the defect one spelling further out
that the row names as the thing this must not become.** ⛔ **The handoff `**Kind:**` line
is the opposite and stays so: every part of it CLAIMS to be a task id, so the undeclared
joiner is refused with its own text in the finding.** ⭐ **Both directions are asserted.**

⛔ **NO NEW BOARD RULE CODE WAS MINTED, and the ground is the row's own clause.** ⚠️ Its
clause 1 is an OR — *the grammar is declared and the reader reads all of it*, OR *the
undeclared forms are refused loudly* — ⭐ **and the first branch is what landed.** ⛔ **A
fourteenth `board-*` rule during the mint freeze, to refuse a spelling no live cell uses,
would have been the wider change taken without the office that owns the register.**

⚠️ **`register.py` stood at R11's ceiling exactly, so `identifiers` was REPLACED by its
delegation rather than added to** — ⭐ the module is smaller than it was, and its
`Depends on` line names `ids` because a dependency nobody declared is one nobody expects.

**Surprises:**
