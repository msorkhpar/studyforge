**1. `content`'s seam is `policy` | `parse`, and it was measured rather than
chosen.** Three things say so and none is taste:

- ⭐ **The 84-line module docstring split along the line without a sentence
  being rewritten to fit.** §"The third state, and why it takes globs where an
  exclusion takes a path" plus *"exclusion wins over inclusion"* is `policy`'s
  whole rationale; §"the asymmetry is the design", *"an exclusion names one
  path"* and §"Rule 1a" are `parse`'s; the C2 paragraph — why this is a schema
  field at all — is the package's. That is the check R11 §3c actually asks,
  answered by the document the previous authors already wrote.
- ⭐ **The consumers already split across it.** Measured at `ddddd05`:
  `manifest/document.py` takes `ContentPolicy` and `parse_content` and never
  classifies; `validate/source/classification.py` takes `Classification` and
  calls `classify` and never parses. Two packages, one on each side.
- ⭐ **The dependency was already one-way in the code.** `parse` builds what
  `policy` defines; nothing in the model knows a manifest was ever a `dict`.
  ⛔ **This is weaker than `W44`'s *neither half imports the other* and it is
  stated as weaker**: a parser must construct its model. What is asserted is
  the *direction* — `test_the_seam_holds_and_the_model_never_reads_the_reader`
  — with a negative control planting the forbidden import as a **relative**
  one, the spelling the clause did not picture.

**2. The gate's seam is bound | tell | claim, and the assertions are two files
rather than one.** Argued above under *What landed*.

**3. `_escape` is re-exported from `content/__init__.py` with a redundant
alias, and `edits.py` was not touched.** ⛔ `manifest/edits.py` imports this
private name and has since W19 unified the two "how does this path leave the

**4. `WILDCARDS` went onto `__all__`; it was public and unexported before.**
