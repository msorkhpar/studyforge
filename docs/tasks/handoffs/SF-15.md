## Decisions

### ⛔ The skeleton gained a SLOT, and that is the designed growth path

⚠️ **The alternative was a `breadcrumb` function nothing ever called.** `render/page/document.py`'s own docstring says *"this is the module the composer grows into: every new page region … adds a slot"*, and the `""`-by-name rule exists so the two peer renderers fail loudly when it does. ⭐ So the cost is four forced one-line edits and a diff wider than a one-file `Owns` — which §10 is explicit about cutting both ways: *"a diff that is too small for its Acceptance is the same defect wearing the other face."*

### ⭐ The slot is placed so that NO GOLDEN MOVED — measured, 0 files

⛔ `${breadcrumb}` sits immediately before `${outline}` with no whitespace between them, and an absent trail renders `""`, so a page with no trail is **byte-identical** to the page before this branch. ⚠️ **Verified by running all three regenerators** (`pages`, `indexes`, `containers`) and reading `git status --porcelain tests/fixtures/`: **empty**. ⭐ This matters for `SF-26`, the row after mine whose job is to freeze rendered bytes: I shifted nothing for it to re-freeze.

### ⛔ A refused href DROPS in the bar and drops only the LINK in the trail

⭐ **Both are Ruling 164, not one ruling and one exception.** Its test is *"has the reader lost a WAY TO GET SOMEWHERE, or has the page lost THE THING IT EXISTS TO SHOW?"* — and a crumb is both at once. ⚠️ Dropping a crumb renumbers the hierarchy on the page: *"Basics › Your first class"* where the material says *"Basics › Getting Started › Your first class"* is a true statement about a **different corpus**, which is worse than an unlinked word. ⛔ So the anchor drops and the words stay, and both branches emit the same text.

### ⭐ `Link(href=None, key=…)` is the fallback, and it is a DECLARED TYPE

⛔ Spelled as `render.index.Item(href=None)` spells it, which is Ruling 164's third row: *a renderer that cannot tell "no page yet" from "bad href" always picks the wrong one of drop-or-raise*. ⚠️ The composed `<index>#<key>` is gated by `safe_href` **as one string, after composing** — gating only the index's half would let a key no href can be spelled with reach the page, which is the half of `W57`'s lesson that was about the *set* rather than the prefixes in it.

### ⛔ `SF-34`'s handed finding is FIXED, not carried

⭐ `LINK_SLOTS` was `("previous", "prev", "← ")` with a `" →"` decided by an `== "next"` test inside `_link`. ⛔ All of it — the two arrows **and** the three `rel` values — is in `link-previous.html`, `link-index.html` and `link-next.html` now, and the rendered bytes did not move. ⚠️ **Three files rather than one with a `${lead}` slot**: a template whose glyph arrives as a substitution has not moved the glyph out of Python, only the markup around it. ⭐ The trail's `›` went into a file on the same argument, rather than into a `::before` rule, because R8's `file://` floor means a page opened with no stylesheet must still read as a trail.

### ⭐ `test_acceptance.py` was split at a seam, not at a line number

⛔ It crossed R11's 600 (**737** lines, and the floor said so). ⚠️ Rather than a `Size exception:`, it divides into `sites.py` — *the directory of pages a build writes, and how to read one back* — and `test_acceptance.py`, *the clauses*. ⭐ `pages.py` and `indexes.py` are the precedent for a helper module beside its tests. ⛔ **`sites.py` writes only under a `tmp_path` a test hands it** and never into `tests/fixtures/pages/`, so it is **not** the fourth writer the epic warns the next author about.

### ⭐ Ruling 187, discharged at the place it actually bites here

⛔ `test_every_outline_entry_points_at_an_anchor_the_page_emits` is asserted in both directions and **a collision satisfies both halves**: two sections keyed `java` both yield `#s-java`, every entry still points at an anchor the page emits, and the rail silently sends two lines to one place. ⭐ Surjectivity was already asserted; injectivity is now asserted beside it, with the collision planted to prove the new check notices.
