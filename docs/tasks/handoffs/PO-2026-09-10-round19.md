### ⛔ `W26` was minted TWICE, and an id space gets ONE MINTER

⛔ **Two different live `W26` rows, from two branches, in one wave — and `git
merge` combined them cleanly with no conflict.** `W26`(PO-18) is `PO-18/2`
(PEP 758, closed, not a defect); `W26`(CTO-18) is Ruling 57, live, Developer 2,
branch `fix/W26-gate-tell`.

⭐ **Round 18 predicted this in terms** — *"worst case both increments merge
cleanly and one number is lost silently"* — ⛔ **and it happened to `W`-ids in the
very wave the warning was written.**

⛔ **But the finding rule's remedy does not transfer, and that is the useful
half.** ⚠️ **Findings were fixed by scoping the number to its document, and
`W`-ids already live in exactly one document.** ⭐ **So the cause is not *no
allocator* — it is *two allocators*: one file, two authors, two branches.**

⛔ **Ruled: an id space has exactly one minter, and it is whoever owns the
document the space lives in.** Rulings → CTO (which is why 57 and 58 are clean).
`W`-ids and task ids → PO. Findings → per-document, already ruled.

⭐ **RATIFIED by Ruling 68, and NARROWED in a way that matters:** ⛔ **minting is
about the ID, never the authority to decide.** ⭐ **C6 locates a ruling by its
artifact, so an unnumbered ruling still binds** — ⚠️ **without that clause every
routing would wait on a CTO round, which is the opposite of the point.**
⛔ **Read my rule with 68's: the CTO was never asked to stop routing, only to
stop numbering.**
⭐ **The CTO loses nothing they were using it for: their round-18 routing was
correct, wanted and urgent — what it did not need was a number.**

⛔ **Neither `W26` is renumbered.** `W4`'s precedent, and the live one keeps the
bare id because a branch, an assignee and an urgency already point at it.
