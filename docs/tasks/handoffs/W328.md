## What landed

### Clause 1 — the rail is flush against the window and stays there

```css
body:has(nav[aria-label="Containers"]) {
  max-width: var(--page-max);   /* was: min(--page-max, --rail + --measure + 5 gutters) */
  margin-left: 0;               /* new — the centred cap is what floated the rail in */
  padding-left: 0;              /* new — `reading.css` puts a gutter on every body */
}
body > nav[aria-label="Containers"] {
  position: sticky;  top: 0;            /* new */
  max-height: 100vh; overflow-y: auto;  /* new */
  border-left: 0;                       /* new */
  border-start-start-radius: 0; border-end-start-radius: 0;
}
```

⭐ **The rail never floated itself in.** `body` carries `margin-left: auto` and
`margin-right: auto`, so the whole page sat in the middle of the screen and the
rail — the first grid track — started wherever centring had put it: measured at
`c79f485`, the rail's border box left edge was **94px** in a 1280px window and
**734px** in a 2560px one, and the body's was 74px and 714px. ⛔ Flush
left is therefore a claim about the PAGE's left margin and the PAGE's left
gutter, and both are given up, while the RIGHT gutter is kept so the reading
column still has air.

### ⛔ `position: sticky`, and NOT `position: fixed` — the hazard the brief names

⚠️ **The brief's second hazard is *"taking the rail out of flow must not orphan
the reading column"*.** ⭐ **Sticky does not take it out of flow at all**: the
rail keeps its grid track, so the column clears it by construction rather than by
an inset that has to be kept equal to `--rail` by hand. A fixed rail would leave
the grid and the prose would lay out underneath it.

⭐ **It is `W326` that made sticky possible, and the join is asserted rather than
remembered.** A grid item's containing block is its grid AREA and sticky cannot
travel outside it, so a rail in one row sticks within that row and moves by
nothing — which is exactly what `W325/3` said. ⛔ **`W325/3`'s reason is DATED by
`W326`'s span and its record stands unedited** (Ruling 106); what `W326/4` left
open — *"whether `position: sticky` then BEHAVES is untested here"* — is measured
now, in a browser, after scrolling.

### ⛔ A rail taller than the window scrolls ITSELF, and that is two declarations

⚠️ **`max-height: 100vh` and `overflow-y: auto` are ONE decision.** The height
without the overflow CLIPS a corpus's last containers; the overflow without the
height never applies. ⛔ **And it is not optional the moment the rail stops
scrolling with the page**: before this row a reader reached the last container by
scrolling the page, and there is no page scroll to reach it with now.

### Clause 2 — the reading column is a function of the viewport

```css
grid-template-columns: var(--rail) minmax(0, 1fr);  /* was: minmax(0, calc(--measure + 2 gutters)) */
```

⭐ **Measured, same page, same built tree:** the reading surface's border box went
**800px → 968px** at a 1280px window and **800px → 1224px** at 2560px. ⛔ **The
running measure is NOT reopened**: `reading.css` still caps `main p, main li`,
`hr`, `blockquote` and `details.disclosure` at `--measure`, and the wide shape
does not mention `--measure` at all — asserted, so it cannot start to.

⭐ **The bound, and what it was chosen against.** An unbounded column puts a code
block across a whole 4K display, which is the full-bleed defect the column
section was written against one viewport along. The ceiling is `--page-max`, the
one the palette ALREADY declares — ⛔ **so this row mints no token and a reader
moves the bound by changing one number in one file.**

## Decisions

### ⛔ THE SCROLL CLAIM IS A MEASUREMENT AND THE ANSWER IS *"IT DID NOT MOVE"*

⚠️ **Read `W328/1` before anything else in this document.** The user's words were
*"longer scroll than needed"*, and the honest reading is that the reading
column's width does not control this framework's page length **at all**. ⭐ What
DOES shorten a page is the rail's new cap, and only on pages whose rail is longer
than their prose. Both readings are in `W328/1` and `W328/2` with their viewport,
their page and the box each was taken from.

### ⭐ The unit clauses are a NEW MODULE, and the seam is the SUBJECT

⛔ `test_chrome.py` answers *which regions `chrome.css` rules and where the
column's one bound lives*; `test_chrome_rail_rows.py` answers *does the rail's
row span cover the page skeleton*. ⭐ **Every clause this row adds is a claim
about the VIEWPORT** — the edge of it the rail sits on, the scroll of it the rail
survives, the height of it the rail is bounded by, the share of it the column
takes. ⚠️ **The ceiling is the other half and it is stated rather than implied:**
`test_chrome.py` was measured at **537 lines against a 600 ceiling** before this
row, and R11's remedy is a split at a named seam, never a trim (Ruling 261).

## For dependents

- ⛔ **`chrome.css`'s wide shape now zeroes `body`'s left margin AND its left
  gutter.** Anything that adds a region to the reading column gets the right
  gutter and no left one; the rail's track is the left inset. A region that wants
  air on the left asks for it, and does not get it from the page.
- ⛔ **`max-height: 100vh` and `overflow-y: auto` on the rail are ONE decision.**
  Dropping either silently loses a corpus's last containers.
  `test_chrome_viewport.py` refuses each one separately, by name.
- ⚠️ **`tests/visual/ACCEPTANCE` has a ninth row** mapping the flush-and-fixed
  clause to `test_rail_fixed`. That mapping is asserted total against the
  package's own test modules, so a browser-driving module without a row is
  refused.
- ⭐ **Before believing that a layout change shortened a page, measure it.**
  `W328/1` is a change that widened the reading column by more than half and
  moved the scroll by nothing at all, and the reason is in this framework's own
  stylesheets rather than in the fixture.
- ⚠️ **A geometry probe taken after a `window.scrollTo` must pass
  `behavior: 'instant'`** — `reset.css` sets `scroll-behavior: smooth`, and the
  reading otherwise describes the page before it moved.
