## ⛔ `W431/1` — the defect the merge gate found, and what it actually was

⛔ **The gate's reading was a symptom of a real defect, and the first two explanations this
office reached for were both WRONG.** They are recorded because each was plausible, each was
tested, and testing is the only reason the third one was found.

⭐ **First, it was reproduced — or rather, it was NOT.** The whole suite was run in the pinned
image on the rebased tree, unchanged: **GREEN**. The visual package alone was run with the
sequence repeated five times: the scroll never moved. ⛔ So the 2px is not deterministic, and
a repair guessed at from the gate's line alone would have been a repair to nothing.

⭐ **So the RESTORE was measured directly instead of waited for**, at five scroll positions
from the top of the page to its end. ⛔ **MEASURED, deterministic, and on the fixture unit
page:**

| the reader left the page at | came back at | moved by |
|---|---|---|
| 0 (the top) | 0 | ⭐ **0** |
| 1200 | 1184 | ⛔ 16px |
| 1400 | 1184 | ⛔ 216px |
| 1490 (the end) | 1184 | ⛔ **306px** |

⛔ **Cause one: the expansion SHORTENS THE DOCUMENT.** `position: fixed` takes the panel out
of flow, so the page loses exactly the panel's own height — **2390px becomes 2084px and the
furthest a reader can scroll drops from 1490 to 1184** — the browser CLAMPS a scroll that no
longer fits, and restoring the height does not undo a clamp. ⭐ **At the top of the page there
is nothing to clamp**, which is why the original check, taken at scroll 0, passed.

⛔ **Cause two, and it is the one that hid: `reset.css` sets `scroll-behavior: smooth` on
`html`, so EVERY scroll animates** — including the one a `focus()` starts when it brings its
target into view. ⚠️ **A reading taken during that glide sees somewhere the reader is only
passing through**, and a glide's position depends on when the reading lands. ⭐ **That is the
2px**: the merge gate read a restore two pixels into an animation that had hundreds to go.
⛔ It is also why a first repair — remember the position, then `window.scrollTo(0, was)` —
**still measured 306px**: the corrected scroll was animating too, and the check read its
first frame. ⚠️ The intermediate hypothesis, that `scrollTo` was clamping against a stale
layout, was tested with a forced reflow and **refuted**; that line is not in the diff.

⭐ **The repair, and exactly one thing now decides where the page is:**

- the position is read **before** the panel leaves flow, and written back **after** it returns;
- the write is `behavior: 'instant'`, so nothing is ever mid-glide;
- focus is moved with `preventScroll: true`, so it is not a second decider.

⛔ **And the CHECK moved to where the defect lives.** A reading taken at the top of the page
says nothing about this, so the new one puts the reader at the END of the page, and it asserts
the document actually shortened under the expansion — without that, a build that never
shortened it would pass over a clamp that never happened.

⛔ **The other half was an assertion this office had written badly, and it is corrected rather
than loosened.** The geometry check compared the scroll after the restore against a reading
taken **before a single Tab press** — so any scroll caused by *reaching* the control was
charged to the restore. ⭐ It now compares against the page the reader actually left, read
immediately before the press. ⚠️ **It is still EXACT equality and there is no tolerance
anywhere in this row**; what changed is which two readings are compared, and the row's clause
is unchanged.

## Decisions

- ⭐ **The expansion is ONE attribute on the section and a rule in the stylesheet.** The panel
  already holds both tabs, Run, Submit, Stop and the output, so *keeping everything* is not a
  feature to build — it is what NOT MOVING ANYTHING gives you for free. ⛔ The clause the row
  is measured by then reduces to a property of the code rather than a promise: a stylesheet
  cannot reparent a node, so the shape that throws the reader's buffer away is not reachable
  from where the expansion is decided.
- ⭐ **Nothing is done to `<body>`.** A scroll lock is the usual companion to a full-viewport
  surface and it is refused: the reader's place in the page is one of the things the restore
  must give back, and the cheapest way to keep it is to touch nothing that holds it. ⚠️ The
  browser reading asserts the document's scroll offset is the same before and after.
- ⭐ **Both of the control's words are the TEMPLATE's.** The button ships showing one and
  carries the other in `data-practice-label`, and the script spells neither. ⚠️ The
  alternative — the pair in `practice.js`, as `TITLES` already does for the tabs — would be a
  second place for a label to drift, and the drift is silent: the control simply stops
  changing what it says.
- ⭐ **The control ships `hidden` and is shown where the run client answered**, beside Run and
  Submit. ⛔ Over `file://` the panel is one sentence, and a control that makes a sentence
  full-screen is the dead button this module refuses everywhere else.
- ⭐ **Escape is read on the DOCUMENT, not on the panel.** Focus may be resting on `<body>`
  when the reader presses it. ⚠️ **And a key pressed INSIDE an editor frame never reaches this
  document at all — which is right, not a gap:** Escape means something of its own in an
  editor, and a page that stole it would be taking a key out of the reader's hands.
- ⭐ **Focus moves to the SECTION on expand** (`tabindex="-1"`, so it is script-reachable and
  never a stop an ordinary page's keyboard reader Tabs through) **and back to the control on
  restore.** That is the standard shape for a surface that covers the viewport, and the row
  asks for both halves.
- ⭐ **`z-index: 3`.** Above BOTH of the page's other raised layers — the skip link at `2` and
  the narration transport at `1` — or the maximised practice is covered by the bar it was
  supposed to make room over.
- ⭐ **The new browser readings are a MODULE OF THEIR OWN and a declared `ACCEPTANCE` clause.**
  [`tests/visual/test_practice_panel.py`](../../../tests/visual/test_practice_panel.py) reads
  the panel AT REST and every clause there counts focus stops; this one reads it across a
  TRANSITION and needs an origin with frames, which would change what those checks read.
  ⚠️ `test_init.py` asserts no undeclared test module exists in that package, so the clause is
  a declaration and not a convenience.
- ⭐ **`serving(windows=True)` is OPT-IN.** A frame is a focus scope of its own; turning it on
  for every origin in that package would have quietly changed every traversal already there.

## Surprises

- ⚠️ **The harness could not frame its own pages, and the reason is correct.** The first draft
  pointed the two windows at two real built pages of the same served tree. ⛔ Every response
  that server sends carries `frame-ancestors 'none'` and `X-Frame-Options: DENY`, so Chrome
  refused both and the frames read as cross-origin error documents. ⭐ Repaired by using blank
  same-origin documents and **saying so where the choice is made** (`served.WINDOW_URLS`);

  is both the real keyboard path and an exercise of `W429`'s own arrow handler.
- ⚠️ **`practice.js` is now at 399 of R11's 400.** ⛔ No exception was taken and nothing was
  relaxed; the file was TRIMMED to fit, twice. ⭐ **The next row that must add to it should
  expect to split it** — `tools/quality/approach.py`'s notice is the instrument that will say
  so. The natural seam is the two frames and their tablist, which is one subject.
- ⚠️ **`tests/studyforge/render/page/test_practice.py` reached 600 in a first draft**, which is
  R11's test ceiling exactly. ⭐ The containing-block census moved to
  [`test_bundle.py`](../../../tests/studyforge/render/pageassets/test_bundle.py), which is

## Findings

- `[structural]` — ⚠️ **A maximised panel with NO editor running stretches its dashed
  "the editor is not running" box to the height of the viewport.** The editor slot is the one
  flexible child of the expanded column, deliberately, because the reader asked for more of
  the CODE; with no frame in it the slot still takes the room and the sentence sits at its
  top. ⛔ Seen and NOT fixed: the states that could tell them apart in CSS alone are `:has()`
  (a capability floor this project has never taken) or a second attribute from the script
  (`practice.js` has one line of headroom). ⭐ It is cosmetic, in a state this control does not
  exist for, and it is a row somebody mints or does not.
- `[local]` — ⚠️ **The reader's site at `:8770` was BUILT before this branch**, so its
  `practice.js` is the previous one and **no maximise control will appear there until the
  corpus is regenerated**. ⛔ Regenerating a corpus is the register's, not an office's, and it
  is filed here rather than done. ⭐ This is the same shape `W430` filed one round ago and it
  has the same remedy.
- `[local]` — ⚠️ **The whole suite took a quarter of an hour and more in the pinned image on
  this host**, against the 57 s this repository's own documents record at `af31fd7`. Several
  containers were up and other offices' suites were running beside it. ⛔ Filed as a
  scheduling observation only; `docker/dev/check`'s outer bound is 1800 s and was not
  approached. ⛔ **And it is NOT an explanation for `W431/1`**: a scroll position is not a
  timing quantity, the defect was reproduced deterministically at five scroll positions with
  nothing else running, and the load hypothesis was never used to excuse it.

- `[structural]` — ⛔ **`scroll-behavior: smooth` on `html` (`reset.css`) makes every scroll
  on every page of this framework an ANIMATION, and nothing in the repository says so where a
  reader of a scroll would look.** ⚠️ It cost this row two wrong diagnoses, and any future
  check or feature that sets or reads a scroll position will meet it the same way: a reading
  taken right after a scroll is a reading of a page still in motion. ⭐ `reading.css` already
  turns it off under `prefers-reduced-motion`, so the mechanism is known — what is missing is
  the warning at the point of use. ⛔ Seen and NOT fixed: `reset.css` is not this row's
  surface. Owner: the register, to route as a one-line row or to decline.
