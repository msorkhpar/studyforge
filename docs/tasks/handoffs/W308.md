## What landed

### ⛔ THE RULE: *one office per merge*, not *every line is a placeholder*

⭐ **A NON-PLACEHOLDER author line is never refused.** ⛔ What is refused is **TWO DISTINCT
OFFICE identities among the commits one merge INTRODUCES** — which is the row's own sentence,
*one office's work landing under another office's name*, and nothing more.

| what a merge introduces | office identities | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| a register round's own commits, under a real identity | `0` | ⭐ passes |
| one office's carrier | `1` | ⭐ passes |
| an office's carrier with a coordinator fixup on it | `1` | ⭐ passes |
| ⛔ a carrier carrying a SECOND office's commits | `2` | ⛔ **REFUSED** |

### ⭐ `tools/authorship.py` — WHO WROTE IT, as a verdict

- `author_is_placeholder(line) -> bool` — whether a line is an OFFICE's, and **the only
  function on the merge path that ever sees an author line.** ⛔ It returns a BOOLEAN.
- `read_authorship(root, branch) -> Authorship` — reads `HEAD..<branch>` OLDEST FIRST and
  reports `population`, `offices` (a COUNT), `crossed` (SHORT SHAS), or an `unread` reason.
  ⛔ **No field holds an author line**, so an `Authorship` can be printed in full safely.
- `render_authorship(authorship) -> list[str]` — the printed lines, **population first**.

### ⭐ `tools/mergegate.py` — the gate, wired BEFORE the merge is staged

`stage_and_read` calls `read_authorship` after the committer-identity check and **before
`git merge --no-commit`**. `Outcome` gained an `authorship` field; `Outcome.verdict` answers
it FIRST and never consults `restored`, because nothing was staged and there is no tree to
restore. `render` prints the authorship reading above the gate readings on every path.

## Decisions

⛔ **1. A SPLIT at a named seam, not a trim** (Ruling 261). `tools/mergegate.py` stood at
`356` of R11's `400` before this row. ⭐ **The seam is the QUESTION ASKED:**
`tools/authorship.py` answers *who wrote the commits this merge introduces*,
`tools/mergegate.py` answers *what the tree that merge produces reads*.

⛔ **2. The population is `HEAD..<branch>`.** The commits a merge INTRODUCES are the
proposal; everything reachable from `HEAD` has landed. ⭐ So the gate is never a backlog over
frozen tips that no office may clear.

⛔ **3. TWO OFFICES, not *no person*, is the refusal** — this replaces the first form's rule
and is the whole of the repair. ⭐ The row's sentence is *one office's work landing under
another office's name*, and **two distinct office identities in one introduced set is that
sentence stated as a property.** ⚠️ *"Every author line is a placeholder"* was a PROXY for it,
and the proxy was false of the coordinator, whose real identity the standing ruling permits.

⛔ **4. It is still not a roster.** This module knows no office's name: it asks whether an
address is unreachable BY CONSTRUCTION (RFC 6761's reserved TLDs, RFC 2606's documentation
domains), and then whether two such addresses DIFFER. ⭐ `W305` refused *"add `dev1` to the
allow-list"* because it fails the next office called anything else; that objection is not
re-earned here.

⛔ **5. The reserved-domain knowledge is DUPLICATED rather than imported, deliberately.**
`tools/quality/personal_data/shapes.py`'s `ALLOWED_ADDRESS` is the same knowledge, and
importing it was refused twice over: that is a FLOOR package, and `tools/mergegate.py`'s
contract says it imports neither `studyforge` nor `tools.quality` so that *a tree too broken
to import is still one whose merge is REFUSED rather than one that crashes the gate.*
⚠️ **The cost is drift, and it is a FINDING below rather than a silence.**

⛔ **6. The AUTHOR line only, not the committer.** The row says *author line*; reading the
committer too would be a silent widening. ⚠️ Recorded so the next office knows it was a
choice and not an oversight.

⛔ **7. The gate runs BEFORE staging**, so a refusal costs no gate and leaves no tree to
restore. ⭐ That ordering is what lets the gate be strict without ever being the reason an
office loses work.
