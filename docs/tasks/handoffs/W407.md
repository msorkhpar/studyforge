## What landed

⭐ **The rule, argued once, in `render/page/anchors.py`'s docstring.** A unit page's `<h1>` and a
source document's opening heading are **one statement**, so the page prints it once — as the
`<h1>` — and the body does not print it again. `title_heading(document)` names the block; nothing
else decides.

⭐ **A TITLE DOMINATES ITS MATERIAL, and that — not its level — is the test.** The opening block
is the page's heading when it is a heading with words in it and **either**:

| clause | the material that forced it |
|---|---|
| **no other heading in that section's material stands at its declared level** | a title has the document under it; a section heading has siblings |
| **it says the unit's title, stripped of surrounding space** | a page cannot print the same sentence twice and call the second one content |

⛔ **A level test was written first and it was WRONG.** Keyed on level 1 it fixed 25 of the
corpus's 38 unit pages and left 13 still reading their title twice, because that corpus writes 25
of its document titles at level 1 and the other 13 at level 2, each with its sub-sections one
level below its own opener. ⚠️ **It was caught by rebuilding the site and counting, not by
reading the rule** — the suite was green at the time, on both framework fixtures.

⚠️ **The peers are counted on the DECLARED level and never the rendered one.** `heading_level`
clamps to `h2..h6`, so a level-1 opener and the level-2 sections beneath it render identically —
a dominance test that asked the clamp would refuse exactly the documents the rule exists for.
Asserted.

⛔ **A heading with nothing in it is never promoted**, whatever it dominates: the page would be
left with a blank `<h1>` and the unit's name nowhere on it.

⭐ **The reading that settles it:** the corpus rebuilt to a temp directory — **38 of 38** unit
pages are now headed by their own material, and **0 of 38** print that heading again anywhere
inside `<main>`. Before this row, 38 of 38 did.

⭐ **Promoted, never dropped, and that is the whole answer to the register's warning.** The block
keeps its text, keeps its `id` (`prose-b0` is now the `<h1>`'s), and keeps its `data-audio`, so
nothing vanishes from the page — not a word, not an anchor, not a clip. `W388/17` said the title
read twice; it now reads once, and the second reading is the one that was removed.

⭐ **What happens to the `<h2>`s beneath it: nothing, and that is deliberate.** Promotion renumbers
no position and re-levels no heading, so a document's `##`/`###` stay `<h2>`/`<h3>` and the page's
outline becomes the document's own outline. The outline simply loses the entry that pointed at the
title above it.

⛔ **The withholding renumbers nothing.** `blocks.render_all(..., omit=…)` skips a block in the
**output** and leaves `enumerate` untouched, because a position is a block's address: a DOM id is
minted from it and a clip is filed under it. A caller that sliced the list would shift every
position after it and the page would ask for the audio of the paragraph before the one it shows.
Asserted directly, and planted (below).

⛔ **The clip had to move with it, and that is why `narration.js` changed.** `tests/.../test_narration.py::test_every_clip_the_walker_mints_reaches_exactly_one_element`
already asserts every minted clip reaches exactly one element — so a promoted heading with no
attribute would have been a file on disk nothing could ever play. The attribute rides on the
`<h1>`, and the transport's scope widened from `#content` to the document so it can see it;
`querySelectorAll` answers in document order, so the heading is still passage one. The player's
gate now reads the heading as well as the body, so a page whose only spoken line is its title
still gets a transport.

## Decisions

- ⭐ **Dominance, not level.** See above: the level rule was written, rendered against the corpus
  and thrown out. The clause that survived is the one property every one of those 38 openers
  shares.
- ⭐ **Promotion, not suppression, and not title-matching alone.** The register named three
  candidates. *Title-matching suppression* does not fix the reported corpus at all (its heading is
  not the unit's words). *Heading demotion* does not fix it either — the heading already rendered
  as `<h2>` under the clamp, so re-levelling it leaves the title read twice. *The page not
  emitting its own title when the document supplies one* is the only one of the three that both
  removes the second reading and takes nothing off the page, so it is the one that landed.
- ⭐ **Only the section that OPENS the page is asked.** A later section's first heading sits
  nowhere near the `<h1>` and is that section's own name — it is what separates it from the
  section above. Consequence, asserted: the depth2 golden page keeps `<h2>Your first class</h2>`
  on its middle section although the page is titled the same, because that page has three
  sections and the middle one would otherwise reach the reader unlabelled.
- ⭐ **The `<h1>` names the PAGE; the unit's title still names the UNIT.** `<title>`, R4's identity
  block, `data-label`, the trail, the contents and the bar between units are unchanged, so a
  corpus's curated name is still what every index says. Asserted.
- ⭐ **`heads_page` is an instruction, not a question `page.section` re-answers.** One module
  decides (`anchors.title_heading`), one prints it (`page.document`), one withholds it
  (`page.section`). Two modules deciding independently is a page that says its title twice or not
  at all.
- ⭐ **The comparison is `strip()` and nothing cleverer.** Folding case, punctuation or numbering
  would start deciding that two different sentences are one; the level clause is what catches a
  restatement whose words genuinely differ.
- ⚠️ **The textual clause is safe here in a way it was not under the rule `page.section` refuses.**
  That module's *"never textual"* note is about **deleting** a heading whose words match. Under
  promotion an author who edits one character gets the heading back **in the body**, which is
  correct: it now says something the page's title does not.

  Nothing vanishes and no title is repeated, so it is outside this row; it is the same question
  the register raised about levels, one document shape further along.
