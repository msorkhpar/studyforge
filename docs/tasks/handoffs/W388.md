## What landed

### 1. A palette on cool slate, with one live accent (stage 2)

⛔ **Cool, never warm.** Every ground and every ink is on the slate scale of the
user's documentation reference: blue is the strongest channel and red the
weakest. Light is slate paper with slate ink; dark is slate asphalt with a
lane-paint ink. `test_identity` bounds the warmth of every ground, ink and rule
at `(red - blue) / 255 <= 0.015` and catches stage 1's four parchment values.

⛔ **Crisp, not dim, and the band is re-argued from the user's own page.** Stage
1 set one band of 4.5:1–9.0:1 for all three inks — WCAG AA as the floor — and the
user called the result *"too dim"*. The route planner measures 12.14:1 for its
body ink, 8.33:1 for its quieter one and 5.59:1 for its faintest, on a ground
that is neither white nor black. So each ink now has its own band around the
reading of the ink it corresponds to: `--fg` 10.0–14.0, `--fg-soft` 6.5–10.0,
`--muted` 4.5–7.0. ⚠️ **The ceiling stays**, because near-black ink on near-white
paper is the other failure and `W362`'s light pair broke it at 14.36:1.

⛔ **No green anywhere.** `--sign` (the Up next slip), `--done` and
`--tok-string` were the three green tokens. The slip and the highlight are now
amber, *done* is the slate ink itself — the road behind you is solid, the road
ahead is dashed — and the string ink is amber. `test_identity` reads the HUE of
every token in both themes and refuses a saturated green, refuted against the
route planner's guide sign and bash ink, the documentation site's emerald, and
stage 1's own olive string ink in both themes.

⭐ **The accent's chroma bound is now a FLOOR, and that is the stage-1
reversal.** Stage 1 capped it at 0.30 and shipped a washed ink blue; a live
accent that is nearly grey is what *"boring"* named. `--accent`, `--sign`,
`--focus` and `--hl-bar` must each be at or above 0.45, and stage 1's two ink
blues are what the check is refuted against. Structure stays quiet by a
different bound: the rules are held to a chroma cap AND to the warmth bound.

### 2. The reader chooses the theme (stage 2)

⭐ **A three-state control in the masthead of every page kind**, written once in
the shared skeleton. *System* is a stored value AND what an absent record means,
so the two can never disagree; a two-state control could only record "dark" or
"not dark", which freezes the page for a reader whose machine turns dark at
sunset.

⭐ **The store is `study-progress.js`'s DISPLAY record**, whose own docstring
declared it independent of the read marks and named a later consumer. This is
its first. Nothing else touches `localStorage`.

⛔ **The flash is prevented in the head, not in the bundle.** Every part of
`page.js` is deferred and runs after the first paint, so a stored dark choice
applied there would show the light page for a frame on every navigation. The
skeleton carries a synchronous boot in `<head>`, which is why a rendered page
now has two `<script>` tags and one of them is inline. ⚠️ The boot spells the
store's key, field and version a second time because it must run before any
bundle exists; `test_theme` reads BOTH spellings out of their own files and
holds them equal, refuted by each of the three changed by one character.

⭐ **`theme-color` stays honest.** The skeleton ships one per system scheme. A
reader who has chosen has made those media queries wrong, so the chosen one
widens to `all` and the other becomes `not all`; choosing *system* restores both
exactly as the skeleton wrote them.

## Decisions

- ⭐ **The bands are per ink, not one band for all three.** One band wide enough
  to hold a body ink and a faint ink is too wide to hold either: stage 1's
  4.5–9.0 admitted 5:1 body text, which is what shipped and what was rejected.
  Each band is centred on the corresponding ink of the user's own page.
- ⭐ **`RULE_CHROMA` moved from 0.12 to 0.20, and it is not a loosening.** Stage
  1's figure was measured against a warm grey. The slate scale is a TINTED
  neutral whose blue channel leads by about a seventh, so 0.12 would refuse the
  reference's own hairlines. The rules gained a SECOND bound instead — the
  warmth bound — and a red hairline is caught by that one at any chroma.
- ⭐ **The control is inside `<header>`, not an eleventh child of `<body>`.**
  `chrome.css` spans the rail over the body's ten top-level positions and the
  unit mirror reads that number back; a sibling would have moved the rail.
- ⭐ **Its rules live in `notes.css`, not `chrome.css`.** `chrome.css` reached
  409 lines against R11's 400. The seam is the four parts' own: `notes.css` is
  what the page tells the reader and what it offers them, and it already holds
  the read control. ⛔ A split at a named seam, never a trim (Ruling 261).
- ⭐ **The control is NOT gated on storage working**, where `read-mark.js` is. A
  mark that is not kept is a lie; a theme that is not kept is still a theme for
  as long as the reader is on the page.
- ⭐ **No per-language inks, and that is a refusal rather than an omission.** The
  brief asked for the route planner's java/python/kotlin inks on code fences and
  language chips. ⛔ Painting them means a fixed list of language names in a
  framework stylesheet, which is R1 — and `code-highlight.css` says in its own
  docstring that the framework ships no corpus's languages. The energy the inks
  were for is carried by the amber accent and a saturated pen case instead. See
  `W388/9`.
- ⭐ **The display face is unchanged.** The brief offered Overpass; the second
  reference uses a plain UI sans and this row's settling clauses are the palette
  and the control. See `W388/8` for what was measured, so the next row need not
  re-derive it.

## Surprises

- ⚠️ **Stage 1's own handoff quoted a figure that its files do not carry.** It
  said the palette that shipped read "15.3:1 in dark", and `15.3` was planted in
  two checks as the refutation. Recomputed from `W362`'s actual file values,
  that pair reads **11.60:1** — inside the new bands. The reading that really
  breaks the ceiling is `W362`'s LIGHT pair, 14.36:1, and that is what the
  refutation now uses. ⛔ The `15.3` was a browser reading of the highlighted
  passage, not of body text on the ground.
- ⚠️ **The rail on the index stopped overlapping the list**, because the masthead
  grew by the control's line and the index's rail is only as tall as the courses
  it names. Stage 1's reading compared the rail with `main` alone and passed on
  the height of a heading; it now compares it with the content COLUMN — masthead
  through list — and asserts the rail starts level with the masthead.
- ⚠️ **`innerText` respects `hidden`**, so the masthead reading in
  `test_no_script` saw the control with scripts on and not with them off, and
  reported the masthead as script-dependent on all eight pages. The control is
  excluded by its hook, not by its words.

## For dependents

- ⭐ **A rendered page now carries TWO `<script>` tags**: the head boot, inline
  and synchronous, and the deferred bundle. Anything counting them, or asserting
  that a page has no inline script, reads two now.
- ⭐ **`<html>` may carry `data-theme`.** Anything resolving a palette token must
  resolve it on the live document, not from `prefers-color-scheme`.
- ⭐ **`window.studyforge.progress.preference('theme')` is a live name** with the
  values `light`, `dark` and `system`. `SF-18`'s transport preferences share that
  record and must not share the name.
- ⭐ **`--sign` is amber in both themes and nothing in the palette is green.** A
  consumer that read green as *done* or *passed* should read `--done`, which is
  the slate ink.
- ⭐ **The contrast bands live in two places that are one sentence apart:**

- ⭐ Stage 1's surface notes still hold: `render.index.render` takes `rail`, the
