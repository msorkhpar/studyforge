## What landed

### The shape chosen is (a), and the presumption is met rather than departed from

```css
@media (min-width: 72rem) {
  body:not(:has(nav[aria-label="Containers"])) {
    margin-left: 0;
  }
}
```

⛔ **That is the whole change to the product: one rule, one declaration, one
value, inside the threshold the file already declares.** ⭐ The two shapes the
row left open were *(a)* the one-column page becomes flush left too and *(b)* the
rail page re-centres inside a wider bound; **(a) is taken**, because it follows
the user's own words — *"the left panel shou go all the way to the left side"* —
and (b) contradicts them.

⭐ **And there is a second argument the brief did not have, which is measured:
(a) is not a new shape, it is the shape this file already had everywhere else.**
Below the threshold a page narrower than its own cap already sits at the window's
left edge, because `margin-left: auto` resolves to zero when there is nothing to
centre inside — measured at 720px, `body`'s border box left edge reads the same
on both page kinds. ⛔ **The wide one-column page was the only centred shape
left in the file**, so this row retires it rather than inventing a third.

### ⛔ What the reader now sees, measured on both pages of one built site

⭐ **MEASURED at `6541eec` and again at this tip**, scripts off, on the `depth2`
fixture built by `write_site`, `body`'s **border box** left edge in **viewport**
coordinates:

| viewport | the index, before | the index, after | a unit page |
|---|---|---|---|
| 720px | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 1280px | 220 | 0 | 0 |
| 1920px | 540 | 0 | 0 |
| 2560px | 860 | 0 | 0 |

⚠️ **The defect GREW with the window**, which is why the browser arm takes the
comparison at two wide widths and not one: a repair that lined the two pages up
at 1280px alone would be a coincidence of one width. ⭐ The index's own masthead
now reads the same border box left edge at 720px, 1280px and 2560px — **that page
does not move at all any more.**

### ⛔ THE LEFT GUTTER SURVIVES, AND THAT IS THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN THE TWO RULES

⚠️ **`W328`'s rule gives up `margin-left` AND `padding-left`; this one gives up
the margin only.** The rail page may give its gutter up because the RAIL is what
sits at the window's edge; a page with nothing to put there would run its
masthead into the screen. ⛔ **Both halves are asserted**, on the stylesheet and
in the browser, so a later edit that copies the rail rule wholesale onto the
one-column page reds by name.

### ⛔ THE SHAPE THAT WAS REFUSED, AND IT IS REFUSED BY A TEST RATHER THAN IN PROSE

⚠️ **A third shape is available and it settles the clause more literally:** give
the one-column page the rail's empty track, so its masthead lines up with a unit
page's and *nothing at all* moves on the crossing. ⛔ **It is refused, for three
reasons and the third is the one that decides it:**

- `chrome.css`'s `:has()` condition exists precisely to keep an empty `17rem`
  column off the page that carries no rail, and reversing that is a decision
  about the index's own design rather than this row's;
- measured at 1280px it would leave a blank strip of a rail plus a gutter — the
  rail's border box reads 272px wide and the gutter 20px, so 292px of a 1280px
  window — down the one page in the site with no rail to put in it;
- ⛔ **and it would make the index's own content jump from one gutter to a rail's
  width plus one AS THE READER WIDENED THE WINDOW.** ⭐ That is this row's defect
  moved from the click to the resize, and a repair that relocates its own defect
  is not one.

⭐ `test_the_one_column_page_takes_only_the_margin_and_none_of_the_rest` refuses
`display`, `grid-template-columns`, `padding-left` and `max-width` on that rule
by name, so the refusal is a red check and not a paragraph.

## Decisions

### ⛔ THE READING MEASURE IS NOT TOUCHED, AND THAT IS ASSERTED RATHER THAN PROMISED

⚠️ **The brief names the measure as an OPEN QUESTION THE USER OWNS and asks
whether this fix changes the gap right of a paragraph.** ⭐ **It does not, on
either page kind.** The one-column page keeps the column section's
measure-derived cap and the two-column page keeps `W328`'s `--page-max`; nothing
here moves a bound, and `test_this_row_mints_no_token_and_states_no_bound_of_its_own`
asserts the new rule's entire declaration list is `margin-left: 0`. ⛔ **So the
air to the right of a paragraph is exactly what it was, and the question stays
the user's.**

⚠️ **What the reader DOES still see differ between the two kinds is the page's
RIGHT edge** — the one-column page stops at its measure-derived cap and the
two-column page at `--page-max`. ⛔ **That is deliberately left alone**: the
settling clause is about where the content STARTS, and widening the one-column
page would be answering the measure question in a diff. Recorded as `W333/1`.

### ⭐ The unit clauses are a NEW MODULE, and the seam is the SUBJECT

⛔ `test_chrome.py` answers *which regions this file rules and where the column's
one bound lives*; `test_chrome_rail_rows.py` answers *does the rail's span cover
the page skeleton*; `test_chrome_viewport.py` answers *what the wide shape says
about the WINDOW*. ⭐ **Every clause this row adds answers a fourth question:
do the file's TWO page shapes AGREE** — which is a claim about a pair of rules
and readable from neither one alone. ⚠️ **`W328`'s and `W326`'s modules are
re-taken by running them and are not edited** (Ruling 214), which is also why
`wide_part` is spelled again here rather than imported: a clause that failed

### ⭐ The browser arm builds with `write_site`, and that is not a preference

## Surprises

- ⚠️ **The narrow shape was ALREADY flush left on both page kinds**, and that was
  not obvious before it was measured: `margin-left: auto` resolves to zero when
  the page is wider than its cap. ⭐ **So this row does not introduce a shape —
  it removes the last place the file disagreed with itself**, and that argument
  is stronger than the one the brief supplied.
- ⛔ **A container page carries a rail in a real build and does not in the visual
  harness's own fixtures** (`W333/2`). The harness's rail-less population is four
  pages of two kinds; the built tree's is one page of one kind.
- ⭐ **`test_chrome_viewport.py`'s totality check reads `":has(" in selector` as
  *conditioned on carrying the rail*, and `:not(:has(…))` satisfies the letter
  while meaning the opposite** (`W333/4`). The rule this row adds is genuinely
  conditioned on the region and passes honestly, but the check cannot tell the
  two senses apart — so this row asserts the SENSE in its own module rather than
  editing `W328`'s.
- ⭐ **Nothing about the page bytes changed**, so the committed goldens under
  `tests/fixtures/pages/` were not regenerated and were not touched. The same
  sentence was true of `W325`, `W326` and `W328`; four layout rows in a row have
  been stylesheet-only, which is what R8's `file://` floor buys.
