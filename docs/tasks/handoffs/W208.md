## What landed

⛔ **The defect, confirmed by planting it back.** `corpus/container/errors.py`
argues for THREE exceptions out of `parse` — its own `ContainerError`, plus
`AddressError` and `PersonalDataLeak`, each an argued pass-through.
`cli/plan/derive.py` caught the first and the third. A container map whose
address is the wrong depth therefore raised out of `plan_for`, and
`studyforge plan` is the command whose entire contract is that nothing raises.

⭐ **`studyforge.corpus.container.RAISES`** is that set, as a tuple, in the
package contract — where a caller already looks, and where `parse` and `load`
are exported from. `derive.py` now catches `container.RAISES`. ⛔ There is no
list left to read partially.

⚠️ **The tuple is in `__init__.py` and NOT in `errors.py`, deliberately.**
`errors.py` depends on nothing, and that is load-bearing: `fields` and
`document` both import it, and giving it imports of `address` and
`archive.scrub` would put those in a module that has no other business with
either. `errors.py` gains a pointer instead.

⛔ **The tuple is measured in BOTH directions**, in
`tests/studyforge/corpus/container/test_init.py`: one document per member
reaches that member from `parse`, and for each of those documents catching the
tuple alone is enough. ⭐ A member nothing can raise fails as loudly as a
missing one, and the parametrisation is asserted to cover the tuple so it
cannot silently narrow.

⭐ **Four behavioural cases in `tests/studyforge/cli/plan/test_derive.py`**: a
wrong-depth address, a leaking note and an undeclared variant each become a
`Refusal` with exit `1` and no exception, and no refusal line carries the
absolute path it was read from (R7 — the crash this replaces printed a
traceback that did).

## Decisions

⛔ **The two other catch sites were left alone, and that is a judgement rather
than scope discipline.** `validate/corpus.py` and `generate/declarations.py`
already handle all three names, and each handles them **differently** —
different finding rules, and one deliberate non-catch under Ruling 58. They
cannot take the tuple wholesale without losing the distinction that makes them
correct, so converting them would be a regression dressed as consistency.

⛔ **`W210` was not touched.** `cli/plan/report.py` is not in this diff.

⭐ **The catch site is asserted on the SOURCE as well as behaviourally.** A
hand-copied list is a second declaration that already drifted once, so
`test_the_catch_list_is_the_readers_own_tuple_and_not_a_copy` reads
`derive.py` and fails on a retyped tuple even if every behavioural case
passes.

## Surprises

⭐ **The manifest reader has the same three-name paragraph and is NOT
defective.** `corpus/manifest/errors.py` argues for `ManifestError` plus the
same two pass-throughs, and `derive.py:121` catches only two of them — but
`manifest.parse` translates `AddressError` into `ManifestError` in `_slug`,
and `Manifest.parse_key`, the method that does let it out, is not called during
`parse`. ⚠️ So that site is complete today by a property of the reader, not by
its catch list. See `W208/1`.

⛔ **`except OSError, UnicodeDecodeError:` at `derive.py:139` is valid**, not a
Python 2 relic — PEP 758's unparenthesised `except` tuple, which this project's
`requires-python = ">=3.14"` allows. Noted because it reads as a syntax error
and cost a hexdump to believe.

## For dependents

- ⭐ **A caller reading `container.json` catches `container.RAISES`**, not a
  list of its own. `docs/conventions/` says nothing about this pattern yet; if
  it recurs on the manifest reader (`W208/1`) it is worth a convention rather
  than a third instance.
- ⛔ **Adding a member to `RAISES` costs a fixture.**
  `tests/studyforge/corpus/container/test_init.py` reaches every member from
  `parse`, so a member nothing can raise fails.
- ⚠️ **A catch site that handles the members DIFFERENTLY should keep its own
  arms** — `validate/corpus.py` is the example, and copying the tuple there
  would flatten two finding rules into one.
