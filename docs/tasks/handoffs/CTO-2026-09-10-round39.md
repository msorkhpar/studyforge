## Ruling 143 — `W57/3` is RATIFIED, and the bound is the test's own comment

⭐ **An author may edit a test outside their `Owns` when that test's own text
names their change as the event it is waiting for.** `SF-13`'s author wrote
`test_a_same_directory_neighbour_survives_in_the_data_and_is_dropped_by_the_page`
to pin a defect and said so in the body. A test written to be inverted is
**owned by the row that inverts it**; leaving it to a later task would have
meant shipping a suite that asserts the bug still exists.

⛔ **Three conditions, all met here, and all three are required:**

1. the test **pins a defect** and its own comment says which;
2. the edit **inverts** the assertions rather than deleting them — the diff
   keeps every original line and flips its sense;
3. it is **disclosed in the handoff as a finding**, not done quietly.

⛔ **This is not a licence to edit a test that merely fails.** A test that fails
because your change broke it is a finding against your change. The distinction
is whether the *test's author* predicted you.

## Ruling 144 — `W57/2`'s reach: R7 has THREE subjects and only two have a sweep

⚠️ **`W57/2` is not theoretical, and I could not leave it at "R7-shaped".** The
end-to-end path exists and I walked it.

`archive/scrub.py` documents its own bound, correctly and deliberately:

> of seven home-path spellings, the path rule refuses all seven, this gate names
> three, and `scrub` rewrites six. ⛔ The seventh — a `home` segment under a
> longer prefix — … `/export/home/<name>/x` and `/var/lib/home/cache/x`

⛔ **So an archive may legitimately carry `/export/home/<name>/notes.html`** —
`scrub` is right not to refuse it, because it cannot tell that form from a cache
path. **Measured, old gate reconstructed from `e309172` and run beside the new
one** (both literals built from parts, so no home path is written to any file):

```text
plain     old=ADMIT   new=REFUSE   '/home/<name>/notes.html'
seventh   old=ADMIT   new=REFUSE   '/export/home/<name>/notes.html'
```

⭐ **`safe_href` was the last gate on that path and it admitted everything
beginning with `/`.** The reach, ruled:

- ⛔ **It does NOT extend to a second lax gate in `src/`.** `safe_href` has
  exactly two callers (`navigation._link`, `text` inline links). The third
  `href=` emission, `navigation.outline`, is not a gate at all — its hrefs are
  built internally as `"#" + section_anchor(key)` / `block_anchor(key, position)`
  over keys `_slug` has already refused if non-slug. **Checked, not assumed.**
- ⭐ **It DOES extend as a class, and this is the row.** R7 has **three**
  subjects — strings entering the **repository** (`tools/quality/personal_data`),
  strings entering an **archive** (`archive/scrub`), and strings entering a
  **rendered page**. The first two have a sweep. ⛔ **The third has never had
  one**, and its only gate was the one that was lax.

⛔ **The rule this leaves standing: where an emitter and a validator encode the
same rule in different modules, the pair is tested against each other, and the
test lives with the validator.** `placement.relative_href` refused to *emit* a
rooted href three modules away while `safe_href` admitted one; both were green,
separately, for the whole of M1.

## Ruling 146 — a sweep asserts its own ROW COUNT, or it is not a sweep

⭐ **This is the sharpest instrument finding of the week and I am adopting it as
a rule rather than a note.** Their first sweep ran **one** row, because `docker`
consumed the loop's stdin — and it **exited 0, printed two agreeing baselines,
and looked healthy.**

⛔ **It defeats Rulings 70, 76, 83 and 123 simultaneously, and that is the
point: every recorded signal was correct.** Exit code, test-count tail, skip
count, three readings — all true of the one row that ran. ⚠️ **No check that
inspects a row's output can detect a row that never existed.**

**So, added to the rubric's sweep clauses:**

```bash
rows=0
while read -r m; do rows=$((rows+1)); ...; done < mutants.txt   # ⛔ and: </dev/null on every docker call
[ "$rows" -eq "$(wc -l < mutants.txt)" ] || { echo "SWEEP RAN $rows OF $(wc -l < mutants.txt)"; exit 1; }
```

⭐ **The count is asserted against the population file, declared before the loop
— never against a number the loop itself produced.** ⛔ And every `docker`
invocation inside a loop is redirected `</dev/null`.

## Ruling 148 — the marker contract does not bind the documents that carry the findings

⛔ **`[none]` may not stand beside a real finding. The check for it exists, is
correctly written, and CANNOT FIRE on a PO or CTO round.**

`tools/quality/handoffs/contract.py` has exactly the right rule:

```text
nones = [(number, line) for number, line in good if MARKER_NONE in line]
if nones and len(nones) != len(good):   ->  "`[none]` stands beside a real finding"
```

⛔ **And `tools/quality/handoffs/__init__.py:254` reads `if kind != TASK_HANDOFF:
continue`.** Every PO and CTO round declares `**Kind:** ruling record`, which
`DOCUMENT_KINDS` says *"owes nothing further"*. **Three readings:**

- **live** — `PO-2026-09-10-round31.md` carries `PO-31/9` `` `[none]` `` beside
  eight `` `[local]` ``/`` `[structural]` `` findings. **Floor: clean.**
- **planted** — flip that one line to `**Kind:** task handoff — PO-31`, change
  nothing else. **Floor: 14 findings.**
- ⚠️ **and the planted reading says something worse than I went looking for.**
  Among the 14 is **`marks no finding`**, plus `a marker is not on its own
  finding line` on **ten** lines. ⛔ **The format PO and CTO rounds write their
  findings in — `- **`PO-31/1`** `[structural]` — …` — is not a format
  `check_markers` recognises as a finding at all.** So even lifting the kind
  gate would not enforce the contract; it would report zero findings on a
  document carrying nine.

⭐ **This is why my own round brief had to state the `[none]` rule to me in
prose. The instrument cannot state it.** ⛔ **`PO-31/9` is therefore NOT a build
failure and I am not rejecting it** — the rule it breaks is unenforced and was
unenforceable. ⚠️ **It is still wrong**: a host-versus-pinned divergence is a
real finding and wants `` `[local]` ``, opening *A NEGATIVE result*.

⛔ **Two rows for the PO**, and they are one subject: bind the marker contract to
`ruling record`, and teach `marker_lines` the bulleted finding form both offices
