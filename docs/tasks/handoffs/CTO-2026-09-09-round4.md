## Ruling 1 — C5 · ⛔ options 1 **and** 2. Option 3 refused.

⭐ **They are not alternatives. Option 1 prevents; option 2 detects; and only
option 2 catches the rule nobody has thought of yet.** The PO's recommendation of
option 1 was right and is not enough on its own.

### Option 1 — mostly already done, and the residue is a rule not a task

Option 1 has two halves and only the first is what people mean by it:

- **(a) the floor exists before parallel authoring starts.** ⭐ **This is already
  true.** M0 is complete: ruff is configured, `tools/quality` is present, the
  container builds. M0 was the one window where the floor was being built in
  parallel with its first users, and that window is closed. ⛔ **So there is no
  wave-0 prerequisite left to schedule, and holding M1 for one would be holding
  it for work that is finished.**
- **(b) the standing rule**, which is what actually generalises:

> ⛔ **A commit that adds or tightens a check brings the whole tree into
> compliance in the same commit**, and the check is never merged in a state where
> any tracked file fails it. ⭐ **No open branch is expected to fix a rule it
> never saw** — the rule-adding commit fixes the tree; open branches inherit
> compliance when they rebase.

This is in `FND-06`, which is the next task to add a rule and therefore the first
to be bound by it. ⚠️ **`FND-06` adds the fifth check to a tree fifteen M1
branches are open against. If its sweep finds anything, `FND-06` fixes it** — it
does not file fifteen findings.

### Option 2 — the real fix, and it was my defect

⛔ **The rubric reviewed `$BASE...HEAD`. That is the branch's own changes, so
every one of those three branches was correctly green — the rubric was asking the
wrong question.**

⭐ **The diff is the branch's; the verdict must be the merge's.** Reviewing the
branch answers *"is this change good?"*; a merge gate asks *"is the result
good?"* — and those come apart **precisely when two parallel tasks are each
correct alone**, which is the only situation C5 occurs in.

So §0a now stands up a trial-merge worktree and §4b runs the suite and the floor
inside it. Three commands, no new tooling, and a merge conflict becomes a review
outcome discovered by the reviewer rather than by whoever merges at the end of
the day.

⚠️ **Option 2 was never cheap *before* option 1** — a trial merge needs a floor to
run. The ordering was always floor first, gate second, and both are now in place.

⭐ **Why this is the half that matters going forward:** option 1 protects against
*style* rules, because style is what we happen to have added. The trial merge
sees **any** rule, fixture, contract or checker introduced on one branch and
invisible to every branch cut before it. M1 adds fifteen tasks that author
contracts, not just modules; the next collision is more likely to be a fixture or
a validator than a line length.

**Option 3 is refused.** A known cost paid fifteen times is not a known cost; it
is a policy of paying it.

## Ruling 2 — ⛔ the meta-finding, which is worth more than C5

The PO's framing is exactly right and I am adopting it as the ruling rather than
softening it: ⭐ **a recorded prediction that was not acted on and then came true
twice more is the most expensive kind of finding this project produces.**
`FND-04`'s finding 10 named the shape, named the three options, and named which
was cheapest. It was filed in the right place, in the right format, and read.

⛔ **The protocol said to write findings down. It never said anyone had to rule on
one.** So findings accumulated and nothing forced a decision — and that is a hole
in the mechanism, not a failure of routing. ⚠️ Blaming the routing would leave the
mechanism intact and guarantee a repeat.

**Fixed in two places, because it needs both an author-side and a reviewer-side
obligation:**

- `agent-protocol.md` — findings are marked **`[local]`** or **`[structural]`**,
  and every `[structural]` one is **ruled, scheduled, or explicitly accepted
  before the next wave begins**. ⛔ "Noted" is not one of the three. The test is
  one question: *would this happen again to somebody else?*
- `review-rubric.md` §8a — ⭐ **the reviewer routes them, in the review**, because
  the reviewer is the last person who reads a handoff while anything can still be
  done about it. ⚠️ And a reviewer who spots an *unmarked* structural finding
  marks it: the author is describing their own scope and is the worst-placed
  person to see that something will recur elsewhere.

`grep -rn '\[structural\]' docs/tasks/handoffs/` is the triage list before a wave.

## Ruling 5 — `consuming.json`: the shape now, the content by its owner

⭐ **Ruled: two versions, and conflating them is the defect the file exists to
prevent.** The PO's correction identified it exactly — G1 restored *which
commit*; nothing said *which version of the promise*.

- **The pin file (R18)** records **which build** a component was at.
- **`provides`** records **which promise** it is making.
- **`consuming_api`** versions the file's own schema.

⛔ **They change at different rates and neither substitutes for the other.** A
component rebuilds constantly without changing its promise, and can change its
promise without a new build. A consumer records the `provides` it was built
against; a mismatch is refused, never migrated (R9).

⚠️ **Everything else belongs to the component that ships it** — E12 for the
toolchain, E13 for narration. Designing the file's content now would be designing
a runtime contract against **zero** implementations, which is the error R1 exists
to prevent, arriving from the other direction. ⭐ What is ruled here is only what
stops **two components inventing two shapes**, which is this seam's actual
exposure: it is the one seam neither agent can inspect from their own side, so a
divergence is invisible until both exist.

The register row is now filled; open rows 5 → 4.

## Surprises

- ⭐ **C5 was my defect, not the plan's.** The board framed it as a
  parallel-authoring hazard and the PO framed the routing as its own to wear.
  Both are true and neither is the cause: **the rubric verified the branch and
  called it a merge gate**, and it would have produced exactly this outcome under
  any sequencing and any routing. This is the fourth defect found in that
  document by using it, and the third that was invisible from reading it.

  Two of them only work for rules you can see coming. M1's fifteen tasks author
  contracts and fixtures, so the next collision probably is not a line length —
  which is why the detection half had to land, not just the prevention half.
