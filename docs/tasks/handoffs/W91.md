## Decisions

### ⛔ 1. An index of ADDRESSES and QUOTES. The `where it landed` column is REFUSED.

⭐ **The row asked for *"number, one-line statement, and the ANCHORED address"* and it gets all
three — except that the statement is the record's OWN SENTENCE, quoted, never a one-line
paraphrase of it.** ⛔ **Ruling 195, minted this hour, is exactly this shape one scale down: a
paraphrase can be kept freshly wrong, a quote cannot diverge, and a pointer resolves at read
time.** ⚠️ **197 paraphrases would have been 197 statements nobody re-measures.**

⛔ **And I decline the round-17 index's third column outright.** ⚠️ **Six of its own 42 rows were
already wrong about where the ruling landed on the day it shipped (it says so itself: *"Six rulings
have not reached an artifact"* plus two withdrawn).** ⭐ **That column is a per-ruling claim about
another document, re-measured by nobody — Rulings 161, 150 and 136 all say the remedy for an
unmaintainable second copy is to remove the subject.** ⛔ **`CLAUDE.md` carries no live state for
exactly this reason, one directory up; an index that claimed 197 landing sites would be the defect
that rule was written over.**

### ⭐ 2. The completeness claim is DERIVED and ASSERTED, and here is what asserts it

⛔ **Three instruments, none of them a typed number:**

1. **The population** is the CONTIGUOUS run from `1` over the numbers written as `Ruling <n>` in
   the CTO round records. ⚠️ **Not `max()`** — round 49 contains `Ruling 9999` as an *impossible*
   control, and the document PRINTS it as outside the series rather than indexing it or dropping it
   in silence (Ruling 191's shape).
2. **`check_rulings_index`** re-derives the whole document on every floor run and fails on any
   difference. ⛔ **So the day a ruling is minted without a row, the build is red** — and the
   remedy is one command, in the finding's own text.
3. **`check_pointers`** resolves all 197 anchors against the headings of the records they name, on
   every run. ⭐ **`0 unresolved` at the merge is therefore a statement about every row.**

⛔ **What the next office does when 198 is minted: `python3 -m tools.quality.rulings`.** ⚠️ **There
is no list to extend and no count to retype; a hand-edit to the document is a finding, not a fix
(R19), and the next run reverts it.**

### ⛔ 3. How the two series are kept apart, asked and answered

⭐ **`R1`–`R21` are the SPEC's rulings; `Ruling 1`–`Ruling 197` are the CTO's.** Three mechanisms,
and they are independent:

- **The population regex is the literal spelling `Ruling <digits>`.** ⛔ No `R7`-style citation can
  match it, in any context.
- **A test plants the other series:** `test_the_spec_series_is_never_read` feeds a record saying
  *"R7 and R11 both bind, and R21 is a register"* and asserts the derivation is EMPTY.
- **The document says it in its own header**, so a reader who arrives at the table cannot merge
  them by accident.

⚠️ **And a third series exists, which is why `SERIES_FIRST_ROUND = 14` is declared rather than
derived:** `CTO-…-m0-readiness.md`, `…-round3.md`, `…-round4.md` and `…-round7.md` each open their
own local `Ruling 1`–`Ruling 5`. ⭐ **The declaration carries an inhabited negative control** —
`test_the_excluded_records_really_do_restart_at_ruling_1` reads the real tree and would fail the
day that measurement stopped being true.

### ⭐ 4. Where the code lives, and why it is inside `tools.quality`

⛔ **My first structure was a sibling package `tools/rulings/`, following `tools.knowledge`. It
