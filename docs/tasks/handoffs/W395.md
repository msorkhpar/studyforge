## What landed

⭐ **What the flow says now that it did not:** when a round mints a row whose clause QUOTES, KEEPS
or ASSERTS something another row of the SAME round may remove, rename or rule away, then either
both rows say so — each naming the other by id and what of it it depends on — or the register
ORDERS them, with the later row's **When** cell naming the earlier. ⛔ One or the other, and never
neither. The subsection names `W381/2` as the measurement and [round 126](../BOARD-ARCHIVE.md#po-round-126) as its reading.

⭐ **And a test reads it.** `tests/test_round_mint_collision_rule.py` locates the section by text
it carries, asserts each of its obligations, and refuses a copy with any one of them deleted — so
a rule quietly softened to *the register may order them* turns the suite red instead of reading
green over half a rule. It also asserts the round it cites RESOLVES to a heading in the archive,
and that the rule sits under **The board** rather than under the review gate.

## Decisions

- ⭐ **BOTH rows, and not only the one that removes.** An office reads ITS row: a note written
  only where the removal happens is invisible to the office whose clause is about to disappear
  underneath it, and that office is the one that loses the work — which is the direction `W381/2`
  actually went.
- ⭐ **The register ordering them is the alternative, not a second obligation.** A round that
  cannot summarise the collision in half a sentence can still make it safe by sequencing, and
  Ruling 75 already established the shape: the **When** cell carries the trace.
- ⭐ **No instrument reads the rows themselves, and the document says why.** Whether one clause
  quotes another row's subject is a reading of two arguments; nothing here reads a clause for what
  it depends on. ⛔ The obligation therefore lands on the ROUND — the only place both rows are in
  one head at one time — and what is asserted mechanically is the one thing that can be: that the
  rule is present, whole, and cites a reading that resolves.
- ⭐ **Each obligation is a FRAGMENT in the test, never a whole sentence**, so the rule can be
  worded better without the test refusing the improvement — but never SHORTER by one obligation,
  which is the failure being guarded.
- ⭐ **No ruling was minted** (the freeze): this is a convention clause with its measurement, in
  the document that already governs minting.

## Findings

- `[local]` **`W395/1`: the mint links of `W381` and `W386` name different rounds.** The row's
  argument says the two were minted in ONE round; the archive's moved bodies carry
  `[the mint](#po-round-124)` and `[the mint](#po-round-125)`. ⛔ Outside my surface (the archive
  is frozen and `docs/tasks/rows/` is the register's), and it does not change the rule: whichever
  round minted the second of them knew about the first. ⭐ **For the register:** if the rule should
  also cover *a row minted against a row still in flight*, that is a widening, not a correction,
  and it wants its own sentence.
- `[structural]` **`W395/2`: nothing reads a row's clause for what it depends on.** The rule is
  enforced by the round's attention. If the register ever wants it mechanised, the cheapest
  instrument is not over prose: it is the **When** cell — a row whose argument names another row of
  its own mint round, where neither row's cell orders them, is a reading the board's own parser
  could take.

## For dependents

- ⭐ **The register, at every mint:** before a round's rows are dispatched, ask which of them could
  remove something another one quotes. The answer goes in both rows, or in a **When** cell.
- ⭐ **Any office opening a row:** if your row names another row of the same round, that is the
  collision this rule exists for — read the other row before writing a line.
