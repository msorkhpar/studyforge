## Decisions

**1. ⛔ THE ADDRESS IS OVER THE REQUEST — `format`, `voice`, `text` — AND NOT
OVER THE AUDIO.** The brief left *what a content address is computed over* to
this office. ⚠️ **A digest of the output is the intuitive answer and it is
wrong**: R10's own carve-out says synthesised audio is not byte-for-byte
reproducible, so an output digest changes on every re-synthesis — a cache that
never hits. ⭐ **§8.2 and `E13` both name the inputs**: *"a segment whose text
and voice parameters are unchanged is never re-synthesised"*. `format` joins
them because the store keeps one file per address and two formats are two files;
today the set has one member, so it changes nothing observable and closes a hole
before a second format opens it. The scheme's own name is hashed first, so
changing *what* is hashed cannot collide with today's addresses.

**2. ⛔ THE ENGINE IS DELIBERATELY NOT IN THE ADDRESS, and the record beside the
bytes is the price of that.** Putting it in would make a move between the CPU
and GPU profiles of **one model** re-synthesise an entire corpus — which is the
opposite of what `NS-03` built. ⭐ **What is owed instead is honesty about what
made a file**, so every artifact carries a small JSON record and a cache hit
reports **that** engine. ⚠️ `NS-03` named both options and told this office to
pick deliberately; this is the pick, and `Narrate-Cached: true` is the header
that tells a caller to read `Narrate-Engine` as history rather than as now.

**3. ⛔ Each field is LENGTH-PREFIXED before hashing.** Concatenation would give
`voice="a", text="bc"` and `voice="ab", text="c"` one address. A one-line
property with a one-line test, and it is the kind of thing that is free now and
unfixable after a corpus is addressed.

**4. ⛔ THE TEXT IS NOT NORMALISED, and the alternative was considered rather
than overlooked.** `chunking` strips its input, so `" Hello "` and `"Hello"` do
produce identical audio and a stripping address would spare one synthesis. ⚠️ **A
normalisation here is a second rule that must agree with the chunker forever**;
if they ever disagreed, two different texts would share an address and one would
be served the other's audio. ⭐ **The error direction chosen is *synthesise twice*,
never *speak the wrong words*.**

**5. ⛔ THE FAILURE REPORT IS IN THE MANIFEST, NOT BESIDE IT** — the brief's
second judgement call. ⭐ **One entry per submitted id, always**, so
`len(segments)` equals what was sent and a caller never diffs two lists to find
what is missing. A second document would need its own fetch, its own version and
its own way of being forgotten; a manifest listing only successes has silent
omissions, which is the *"check that can be skipped"* shape §8.2 rejects for
stale clips.

**6. ⭐ TWO ENGINE FAILURES, AND ONLY ONE HALTS THE JOB.** The split is
`engine/errors.py`'s own and this is the first caller to act on it: a bad answer
says nothing about the next segment, so `engine_failed` fails one entry and the
job continues; ⛔ **nothing answering at all predicts the next three thousand
timing out**, so `engine_unavailable` stops the run and the rest are `skipped`
with that reason. ⚠️ **This is not a weakening of *"does not discard successful
work"*** — measured above, run 6 returned six cached artifacts *while the engine
was down*, and run 7's retry paid only for what was left.

**7. ⚠️ A well-formed job always answers `200`.** The job ran; per-segment
outcomes are data in `counts` and `ok`. ⛔ **A caller must read the manifest
anyway to learn the addresses**, so there is no path on which the outcome is
missed — whereas a status code that switched on the *mix* would give a caller
two code paths for one piece of information. A body that is not a job never runs
and is a `400`, including a **duplicate id**, because a manifest keyed by id
cannot report two outcomes under one key without one being invisible.

**8. ⭐ `POST /v1/speech` BECAME IDEMPOTENT TOO, and that is a decision, not a
side effect.** The address is a property of the *artifact*, not of the route, so
one store with two addressing schemes would be a split brain: the same text sent
to the two routes would be two files. ⛔ **`NS-01` pointed at exactly this**
(*"replacing `mint_id()` with a content address is your task's whole storage
change"*). It also changes what `/v1/speech` promises, which is part of why
`provides` moved.

**9. ⛔ `provides` 1 → 2, sourced ONCE from `narrate.PROVIDES`.** Ruling 330(b):
the manifest is a response body, takes no §R9 row, and is versioned by the
promise register. ⭐ **So the manifest carries `"provides": 2` and `/healthz`
reports it** — the *same* key's value on the wire, never a second version number
— and a test asserts the constant and `consuming.json` agree. ⚠️ **`manifest_api`
was deliberately not minted**, and a test asserts its absence.

**10. ⛔ `keep_audio=False` on the batch path is a correctness bound, not an
optimisation.** A job of three thousand segments holding every artifact's bytes
to describe them is measured in gigabytes. A cache hit reads the **record** and
never the audio, which is what makes run 2 cost 0.01 s.

**11. ⚠️ The 4 MiB body cap was NOT raised for jobs.** A corpus larger than one
job is submitted as several, which costs nothing precisely because the addresses
are content addresses — a segment in two jobs is synthesised once. ⭐ **Raising a
memory bound is a worse trade than a caller splitting a list**, and splitting a
list is nothing like the stitching this service exists to keep off callers.

**12. `_ID_PATTERN` widened to 32 **or** 64 hex, still closed.** `NS-01` asked
for exactly that. ⭐ **32 stays legal so an id a caller recorded under 0.1.0 keeps
fetching**; nothing mints one any more, and such an artifact carries no record
so it is never a cache hit — asserted.

**13. The running-server fixture moved to `tests/conftest.py`.** Two modules need
it now and a duplicated thirty-line fixture is how two copies come to disagree
about what a running service is. ⭐ **It also bought R11 headroom**: `test_server.py`
went 413 → 395 while gaining tests.

**14. ⛔ `python3 -m tools.workspace record` was NOT run**, for the reason both
predecessors measured: it rewrites the commit of **every** `present` row, which
would advance the `ISO-8583-jPOS-tutorial` pin the board holds `pending` and
`docs/conventions/workspace.md` forbids advancing. One row was edited by hand.

**15. ⚠️ The per-artifact record is versioned internally and I did NOT mint a
published contract for it.** It is a file with one writer and one reader, both
inside this component; nothing outside reads the store directory. See
`NS-02/5` — the judgement is routed rather than assumed.

**`NS-05` (framework client) — you are the first real caller.**

- ⛔ **Gate every segment before the request. This service still does not**, and
  a batch makes it worse, not better: one leaking segment in three thousand is
  one mp3 nobody can grep for.
- ⭐ **Submit jobs, not `/v1/speech` calls.** 51 segment submissions cost 15
  syntheses here purely because of the cache; per-request overhead is the
  difference the row names.
- ⭐ **You can compute an address WITHOUT calling the service.** `api.cache` in
  `consuming.json` and `addressing` in every manifest carry the scheme, the
  hash, the field order and the exact encoding
  (`sha256` over `"narrate-address-1"`, `format`, `voice`, `text`, each
  length-prefixed as `<bytes>:`). ⚠️ **That is the whole of what `SF-17` needs to
  decide what to submit** rather than submitting everything.
- ⛔ **Read `counts`, not the status code.** A well-formed job is `200` whatever
  its segments did. `ok` is `counts.failed == 0 and counts.skipped == 0`.
- ⛔ **Treat `skipped` as *retry this*, and `failed`/`not_synthesisable` as
  *your input is wrong*.** A job that comes back with `skipped` entries means
  the engine went away mid-run; re-submit the identical job and the finished
  segments are free.
- ⚠️ **The 4 MiB body cap applies.** Split a large corpus across jobs; overlap
  costs nothing.
- ⛔ **Record `Narrate-Engine` / the manifest's `engine*` fields at the moment
  you fetch**, and read them as *what made this file*. On a cache hit they are
  the engine of the **first** synthesis, which is the honest answer and may not
  be the one deployed.
- ⚠️ **Nothing is garbage-collected.** An edited segment's old artifact stays
  under its old address. Reconciliation is yours (§8.2 says only for the units
  the run actually read).
